Why it matters

Heritage is how a civilisation remembers itself

India's heritage is a living inheritance: monuments in stone, but also songs, festivals, recipes, crafts and ideas that millions of people practise every day. Protecting it keeps our roots, our livelihoods and our shared knowledge alive for the next generation.

FAQ

Good to know

Is registration free?

Many events are free. Paid events show the ticket price on the card and detail page. This demo does not process real payments.

Where is my registration stored?

In your browser's Local Storage — it stays on your device and is available under My Tickets.

Can I cancel a registration?

Yes. Open My Tickets and choose Cancel. The seat is released immediately.

Are these official festival events?

No. These are sample student-project events inspired by real traditions. Festival dates (Diwali on 8 Nov 2026, Kartik Purnima on 24 Nov, Konark Festival on 1–5 Dec, Makar Sankranti on 15 Jan 2027) are real; the organisers, venues and artists are fictional.

Is the site accessible?

It uses semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, ARIA labels and respects reduced-motion settings.

Why Indian heritage matters

Heritage is what a society inherits, lives with and chooses to pass on: temples and texts, dance and drumbeats, festivals, food, crafts and philosophies. This guide explains what it includes, why it is worth protecting and how you can take part.
